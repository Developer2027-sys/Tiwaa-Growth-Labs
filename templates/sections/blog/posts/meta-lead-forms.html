Most real estate teams have the same complaint about Facebook and Instagram ads: the leads are cheap, but nobody picks up, and even fewer come for a site visit. The problem is rarely the platform. It is usually how the lead form is set up and what happens in the first hour after someone fills it in.

This guide walks through the exact setup we use for real estate marketing clients across Delhi NCR. Follow it and you can bring down your cost per site visit, often without spending a rupee more.

Why cost per lead is the wrong number

Cost per lead (CPL) tells you how cheaply you collected phone numbers. It says nothing about whether those people can afford the project, want the configuration you are selling or plan to buy this year. The number that matters to a developer is cost per site visit:

Cost per site visit = Ad spend ÷ Site visits

Campaign B looks twice as expensive on a CPL report, yet it brings almost double the site visits for the same money. If your agency only reports CPL, ask for cost per site visit instead.

Step 1: Choose the "Higher intent" form type

When you create an instant form in Meta Ads Manager, you can pick More volume or Higher intent. More volume makes the form quick to submit, which is great for a contest and poor for a ₹1.5 crore apartment. Higher intent adds a review step where the buyer checks their details before submitting, which filters out accidental taps and wrong numbers.

Tip

Turn off auto-filled answers for your custom questions. Buyers who have to choose their own budget are telling you something real.

Step 2: Ask three qualifying questions

Every extra question lowers the number of leads a little and raises their quality a lot. For property, three multiple-choice questions do most of the work:

  1. Budget. Use ranges that match your inventory, for example "Under ₹1 Cr", "₹1–1.5 Cr", "₹1.5–2 Cr", "Above ₹2 Cr".
  2. Configuration. 2 BHK, 3 BHK, 4 BHK or villa, so sales can open the right floor plan on the first call.
  3. Buying timeline. "Within 3 months", "3–6 months", "Just exploring". This one question helps your team decide who to call first.

Keep them multiple choice. Short-answer fields slow buyers down and come back full of blanks.

Step 3: Put the project facts on the intro and review screens

Use the form's intro screen to state the starting price, location and possession timeline, along with the RERA registration number. A buyer who sees "3 BHK from ₹1.6 Cr, Sector 65" and still submits is far more likely to visit than one who submitted for a vague "luxury homes" offer.

The same rule applies on your project landing page: price, floor plans, location map and RERA details above the fold.

Step 4: Call every lead within five minutes

A buyer who fills in your form is often comparing three or four projects that same evening. The first developer to call usually gets the site visit. Waiting until the next morning to work through a spreadsheet means most of your best leads have already spoken to someone else.

Step 5: Follow up on WhatsApp straight away

Not every buyer picks up an unknown number. Send an instant WhatsApp message with the brochure, price sheet and two site-visit slots, and many will reply in writing instead. A good first message looks like this:

Hi Rohit, thanks for your interest in Sector 65 Residences. Here is the brochure and price sheet for the 3 BHK 📄 Would you like to see the sample flat this Saturday? We have 11 AM or 4 PM free. 4 PM works 👍

Step 6: Send site visits back to Meta

By default, Meta optimises for whoever is most likely to submit a form. That is not the same as whoever is most likely to visit. When your CRM sends lead stages such as "contacted", "site visit done" and "booked" back to Meta through the Conversions API, you can switch the campaign goal to conversion leads. Meta then looks for more people like the ones who actually came to the site office.

This takes a few weeks of data to work well, so start sending stages from day one of the campaign. Our analytics and CRM setup does this automatically for LeadsPulse clients.

Step 7: Use creative that qualifies buyers

Your ad does half the qualifying before anyone opens the form. Three things work consistently for property:

  • Show the price. "From ₹1.6 Cr" in the first frame turns away people who were never going to buy.
  • Show the real home. A short walkthrough film or Reel of the sample flat answers the questions that otherwise stop a visit.
  • Name the location. Locality and landmarks, such as "5 minutes from Golf Course Extension Road", attract buyers who already want that area.

Pair Meta with Google Search ads for buyers already searching by locality, and you cover both discovery and intent.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Optimising for the cheapest lead. It rewards forms that unqualified people fill in.
  • One form for every project. Buyers for a ₹60 lakh 2 BHK and a ₹5 crore villa need different questions.
  • No follow-up on weekends. Saturday and Sunday are when most buyers want to visit, so someone must answer.
  • Turning off ads too early. Give a new campaign about two weeks of data before judging it on site visits.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good cost per site visit for real estate in India?

It depends on the price band and location. Mid-segment projects in Delhi NCR often see a few thousand rupees per site visit, while luxury and NRI campaigns cost more. Track your own number weekly and aim to lower it month on month rather than chasing an industry average.

Are Meta lead forms better than sending people to a landing page?

Lead forms usually bring more leads at a lower cost because buyers never leave Facebook or Instagram. Landing pages bring fewer but often warmer leads. Most launches do best with both: lead forms for reach and a fast project page for buyers who want floor plans and prices first.

How many questions should a real estate lead form have?

Three multiple-choice questions work well: budget, configuration and buying timeline. Each extra question reduces lead volume slightly but improves quality. Avoid long short-answer fields.

How fast should my team call a new property lead?

Within five minutes if possible. Buyers often enquire about several projects at once, and the first team to call usually books the site visit. A CRM that assigns leads and sends instant alerts makes this practical.
